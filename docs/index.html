然而，由于北京试图干涉台湾选举所造成的威胁越来越大，它已成为房间里的大象。

中国企图颠覆台湾地方选举并控制台湾国内政治并不新鲜。北京破坏台湾11月选举的战略依赖于恐吓和利诱——通过将台湾的治理定性为“不稳定”来使台湾国民政府失去合法性，在台湾周围举行军事演习以操纵公众看法，为支持与中国统一的候选人提供竞选资金，以及为在中国的台商返回台湾投票支持中国的候选人提供机票补贴。

台湾陆委会副主任沉玉中警告说，北京在人工智能（AI）和人工智能生成的深度造假方面的进步使得“渗透方法变得越来越复杂”。人工智能现在已经成为操纵选举的工具，使得“更容易产生误导性内容”并进行影响力操作。

美国和中国避免人工智能危机的时间可能已经不多了。许多构建世界上最强大的前沿人工智能模型的研究人员和工程师相信，他们可能只需要一两年的时间就能实现递归自我改进——人工智能系统能够构建更智能的自身版本。一旦跨过这个门槛，模型可能会迅速发展超出人类的控制范围。尽管递归自我完善的潜在时间表和后果存在激烈争论，但美国的任何人工智能战略都必须认真考虑其即将到来的可能性。

MATT SHEEHAN is a Senior Fellow at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace and Co-Director of its China AI Initiative.

SCOTT SINGER is a Fellow at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace and Co-Director of its China AI Initiative.

Time may be running out for the United States and China to avert an artificial intelligence crisis. Many of the researchers and engineers building the world’s most powerful frontier AI models believe they may be only a year or two away from achieving recursive self-improvement—the ability of AI systems to build smarter versions of themselves. Once that threshold is crossed, models could quickly develop beyond human control. Although the potential timeline and consequences of recursive self-improvement are hotly debated, any U.S. strategy on AI must take seriously the possibility that its arrival could be imminent.

But in Washington, concerns about the dangers of a near-term “intelligence explosion” are colliding with the geopolitical reality that the United States is locked in a fierce AI race with China. Many policymakers oppose more regulation because they fear it will cause the United States to fall behind. Indeed, many frontier labs continue accelerating the automation of AI development, despite warnings by their own employees that slowing down the pace of progress might be necessary to make the technology safe.

The United States urgently needs a strategy to navigate this dilemma. Yet national security experts, AI researchers, and elected officials fundamentally disagree about what Washington should seek to achieve and how to manage competition with Beijing.

Thus far, three competing visions have emerged. The first seeks a full U.S. victory, or what is often called decisive strategic advantage, over China. Proponents of this perspective believe that the U.S.-Chinese AI race has a finish line: the achievement of artificial superintelligence, or AI with capabilities that far exceed those of humans across nearly all domains. In this worldview, safety is best achieved through dominance, and the United States needs to win the AI race to be able to impose necessary safeguards around the world. A second vision, which we call “AI safety in parallel,” recognizes the need for risk mitigation but proposes that much of this can happen independently on each side. Thus, both China and the United States should pursue their own safety measures—and share best practices with each other—while they continue to compete to advance the technology. A third view argues that AI safety can only be assured by institutionalized cooperation between the United States and China. In this vision, the two countries need a binding international treaty to constrain dangerous AI development.
