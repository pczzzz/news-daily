安德鲁·纳西奥斯 (ANDREW NATSIOS) 是乔治·W·布什 (George W. Bush) 政府期间美国国际开发署署长和总统苏丹特使。他是《枪炮不够：外国援助符合国家利益》一书的作者，本文改编自该书。

在所谓的美国政府效率部削减官僚主义的努力所造成的所有毁灭性后果中，最严重的莫过于美国提供对外援助能力的破坏。 《柳叶刀》的一项综合研究显示，2001 年至 2021 年间，美国国际开发署的援助避免了超过 9000 万人死亡。波士顿大学流行病学家布鲁克·尼科尔斯使用死亡率模型估计，2025 年美国国际开发署的解散在大约一年内导致了超过 70 万例超额死亡。今年五月发表在《科学》杂志上的一项研究发现，美国国际开发署关闭后，失去该机构大量援助的地区的武装冲突和骚乱明显增加。与此同时，发展经济学家马塞拉·尼科利尼和法比奥·萨巴蒂尼表示，以前享受美国大量援助的地区的经济活动急剧下降。

如果没有有效的对外援助计划，任何国家都不可能成为强国。但今天，美国却没有这样的一个。在美国国际开发署 90% 的项目被终止、90% 的工作人员被解雇后，国会正式重新拨款大部分专门用于美国对外援助的预算资金。但管理和预算办公室 (OMB) 尚未发放所有拨款。更具破坏性的是，美国国际开发署的援助资金支出机制已被拆除。根据皮尤研究中心的数据，2023-24 财年和 2024-25 财年美国每年的对外援助支出减少了 260 亿美元；截至 2025-26 财年末，用于对外援助的拨款中有 25 亿美元尚未使用。

OMB 主任拉塞尔·沃特（Russell Vought）自称是一名基督教民族主义者，他阻碍了与支持民主相关的项目支出，并坚持认为人道主义拨款只能发送给两个特定的基督教非政府组织。与此同时，美国国务卿马可·卢比奥宣布，他希望停止向美国国际开发署的开发承包商、大学和当地民间社会组织提供资金，而不了解其他援助资金选择的缺点，例如预算支持，这些选择的责任性要低得多，是美国特有的，而且注重结果。

鉴于美国国际开发署的结束所造成的痛苦，更令人不安的是 DOGE 削减浪费和提高透明度的目标尚未实现。美国总统唐纳德·特朗普的 2025 年“美丽大法案”抹去了 DOGE 削减对外援助所带来的任何微薄节省。而将美国国际开发署的职能转移到国务院的决定，导致美国对外援助资金中的 38 亿美元转入联合国人道主义事务协调办公室，并通过谅解备忘录将 159 亿美元直接转入外国政府财政部。国务院的业务系统薄弱，管理援助项目的人员不足。

这些政府明智地花钱并产生成果的能力往往有限。过去，美国国际开发署监察长办公室严厉批评了此类选择，因为几乎不可能对转移进行审计。参与这些协议的国家中，超过 90% 的透明国际清廉指数得分低于 50 分（满分 100 分），表明存在严重的系统性腐败。这也许可以解释为什么国务院政治任命人员对这些卫生协议的文本保密。

Among all the ruinous consequences of the so-called U.S. Department of Government Efficiency’s efforts to cut bureaucracy, none has been as profound as the destruction of the United States’ ability to deliver foreign aid. A comprehensive Lancet study shows that the U.S. Agency for International Development’s assistance prevented more than 90 million deaths between 2001 and 2021. Using a mortality model, Boston University epidemiologist Brooke Nichols has estimated that the 2025 dismantling of USAID contributed to more than 700,000 excess deaths over roughly a year. A study published in Science this May found that after USAID’s shutdown, places that had lost large amounts of the agency’s assistance experienced a marked uptick in armed clashes and riots. The development economists Marcella Nicolini and Fabio Sabatini, meanwhile, showed that economic activity in areas that formerly enjoyed substantial U.S. aid had dramatically declined.

No country can be a great power without a functioning foreign aid program. But today, the United States does not have one. After 90 percent of USAID’s programs were terminated and 90 percent of its staff members were laid off, Congress formally reappropriated most of the budgetary funding dedicated to U.S. foreign aid. But the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) has not released all of this appropriated funding. More damaging still, USAID’s mechanisms for spending aid money have been dismantled. According to the Pew Research Center, annual U.S. foreign aid disbursements fell by $26 billion between the 2023–24 and 2024–25 fiscal years; $2.5 billion in appropriated funding designated for foreign aid went unspent by the end of the 2025–26 fiscal year.

Russell Vought, OMB’s director and a self-identified Christian nationalist, has stonewalled spending for programs related to supporting democracy and insisted that humanitarian grants can be sent only to two specific Christian nongovernmental organizations. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, meanwhile, announced that he wanted to end funding to USAID development contractors, universities, and local civil society organizations without understanding the downside of other options for aid dollars, such as budget support, which are far less accountable, uniquely American, and results-oriented.

Given the suffering created by the end of USAID, it is even more troubling that DOGE’s objectives to slash waste and boost transparency have not been realized. U.S. President Donald Trump’s 2025 “big beautiful bill” erased any modest savings won by DOGE’s cuts to foreign aid. And the decision to move USAID’s functions to the State Department, which has weak business systems and inadequate staff to manage aid programs, has thus far led to the transfer of $3.8 billion in U.S. foreign aid money to the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs and $15.9 billion directly into foreign governments’ treasuries through memorandums of understanding.

These governments often have limited capacity to spend the money wisely and produce results. In the past, USAID’s Office of the Inspector General has harshly criticized such options because it is nearly impossible to audit the transfers. Over 90 percent of the countries involved in these agreements score below 50 (out of 100) on Transparency International’s Corruption Perceptions Index, indicating serious systemic corruption. A new biography explores the intimate origins of Hans Morgenthau’s hard-boiled worldview.

Some of the most enduring theories of power were born in its absence. Thucydides, exiled from Amphipolis for failing to ward off the Spartans, surmised that “the strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must.” Thomas Hobbes, forced to flee an England consumed by civil bloodshed amid an ailing monarchy, argued that only absolute authority can tame humanity’s primal “state of nature.” Machiavelli, tortured and driven from political life by the Medicis, opined that successful rulers must sometimes be ruthless. Circumstances vary, but when the right observer falls into the pits of powerlessness, they can see in human nature what others cannot.

This is a motif that features heavily in David M. Sacks’ The Realist: Hans Morgenthau and the Purpose of American Power , a meticulously researched biography of the man who made international relations theory a field in its own right—by putting power at its theoretical core.

The Realist: Hans Morgenthau and the Purpose of American Power , David M. Sacks, Henry Holt & Co., 528 pp., $36.99, November 2026

The Realist starts with a cold open: summer 1922 in Coburg, Germany, on a day Morgenthau would later describe as the worst of his life. A high school junior ranked second in his class, Morgenthau was slated to address his peers as part of a celebratory academic tradition. “But what should have been a triumph quickly turned to humiliation,” writes Sacks. The townspeople turned their backs, spat on Morgenthau, and barraged him with antisemitic vitriol for having the gall to participate in a “Christian Day” ceremony. All because, in their eyes, he was no more than a “stinking Jew.”

While that day would leave an indelible scar on Morgenthau, it wasn’t his true awakening. Later that year, he would attend one of Hitler’s gatherings in Munich to witness with his own eyes the antisemitic tide said to be sweeping across all of Germany. It was “one of the most profound experiences of my life,” Morgenthau recalled. Soon after, the same scene played out in his own backyard: In defiance of local law, the führer sallied into Coburg with 800 stormtroopers and delivered two fiery philippics, whipping up a public paroxysm of threats, assaults, and mass street brawls, with tacit support from town police.

这可能是一个热门观点，但我坚信人工智能生成的所有代码都应该是开源的。原因如下。

首先，人工智能模型是在 RMS 宣布 GNU 项目和自由软件运动以来编写的大量开源代码上进行训练的。该代码只是从互联网上抓取并在未经许可或适当归属的情况下使用。这些模型还接受了 Stack Overflow 和 Stack Exchange 姐妹项目的培训，这些项目是根据知识共享许可发布的，法律上要求归属。将这些集体知识转化为专有的、闭源代码感觉就像洗许可证。

第二个原因是合法的：纯粹人工智能生成的代码不是由人类编写的，这意味着它不应该受到版权保护。美国这一原则的基础可以追溯到著名的猴子自拍纠纷，该纠纷规定非人类不能拥有版权。美国版权局后来引用了有关人工智能的先例。遵循相同的逻辑，维基共享资源要求所有纯人工智能生成的图像都被标记为公共领域。

然而，全球各地的版权法有所不同。例如，英国等国家对计算机生成的作品给予有限的版权保护。为了使所有司法管辖区的事情公平，并尊重使人工智能成为可能的开放生态系统，开发人员和公司应在开源许可下明确发布人工智能生成的代码，或使用知识共享零（CC0）许可将其奉献给公共领域。

当然，代码并不总是 100% 手动或 100% 自动化。然而，开发人员越来越依赖人工智能，甚至不检查生成的输出，更不用说修改它来满足他们的特定需求。像这样的代码，除了编写提示之外，人类几乎不付出任何努力，绝对应该是开源的。

This may be a hot take, but I strongly believe that all code generated by AI should be Open Source. And here's why.

First of all, AI models were trained on a massive corpus of Open Source code written since RMS announced the GNU Project and the Free Software Movement. That code was simply scraped from the internet and used without permission or proper attribution. The models were also trained on Stack Overflow and Stack Exchange sister projects, which are released under Creative Commons licenses that legally require attribution. Turning this collective knowledge into proprietary, closed-source code feels like license laundering.

The second reason is legal: purely AI -generated code is not written by a human, meaning it should not be copyrightable. The foundation for this principle in the US goes back to the famous Monkey Selfie dispute , which established that non-humans cannot hold copyrights. A precedent later cited by the U.S. Copyright Office regarding AI . Following the same logic, Wikimedia Commons requires all purely AI -generated images to be tagged as Public Domain .

However, copyright laws differ across the globe. For instance, countries like the UK grant limited copyright protection to computer-generated works. To make things fair across all jurisdictions, and to honor the open ecosystem that made AI possible, developers and companies should explicitly release AI -generated code under Open Source licenses or dedicate it to the public domain using the Creative Commons Zero (CC0) license.

Of course, code is not always 100% manual or 100% automated. However, developers more and more rely on AI without even reviewing the generated output, let alone modifying it to fit their specific needs. Code like this, where humans put little effort, except writing prompts, should definitely be Open Source.
