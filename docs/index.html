ELIZABETH DICKINSON is Deputy Director for Latin America at International Crisis Group.

On September 21, José Luis Pérez Villanueva, better known as “El Cholo”—the second-in-command and financial architect of the criminal group Autodefensas Conquistadores de la Sierra Nevada—was killed during a Colombian police operation on the rural outskirts of Santa Marta. Within hours, militants from that organization launched a dramatic campaign of terror. The group, known colloquially as Los Pachenca, demanded that businesses across the city shutter. Some 90 percent of shops did, as videos circulated of gunmen shooting up storefronts that stayed open.

For two decades, Los Pachenca has been the unquestioned criminal authority in the beachfront city and the adjacent Sierra Nevada mountain range, charging extortion payments from businesses and residents and controlling the flow of drugs out of the Port of Santa Marta, en route to Europe and beyond. But the level of control the group exhibited during the strike was particularly jarring, given that Santa Marta is the vacation home of many of the country’s elite and a common destination for international visitors. Colombia’s government responded by militarizing the city, sending in hundreds of soldiers and police officers, who rolled into town on the main highway with tanks and air support. Many shops still stayed closed until the morning of September 25, when the group announced that it was ending the strike. For locals, Los Pachenca’s reaction was a message about who remains in charge.

Calm has since returned to Santa Marta, but the incident may be indicative of risks to come. Colombia’s newly elected president, Abelardo de la Espriella, has promised a steep military escalation against illegal groups, which have grown precipitously in recent years. As he does so, armed and criminal organizations will undoubtedly respond. Often, their target will not be the state. Instead, they will likely direct their violence against the civilian populations, whom they have spent years coopting and coercing. That is because armed and criminal groups across the country have learned that their true leverage is their ability to govern local communities—and to intimidate and punish them into silence. Even when they do attack state forces, their use of weapons such as improvised explosives and makeshift drones means that bystanders often suffer. In one recent incident in the Pacific coast department of Valle del Cauca, four drone attacks launched by an armed group in a single day left five civilians injured.

These dangers are particularly acute as de la Espriella’s pledge of a zero-tolerance crackdown against armed and criminal groups has won enthusiastic support from Washington. There are indeed a variety of methods, but rather than designing something from scratch, they are mostly harnessing the intelligence coming from a much simpler and less interpretable process: training a compressor (or, equivalently ⁠ (opens in a new window) , predictor) on a very large corpus of data. 1

I believe tracking the pace of progress on training better compressors is key to understanding the arc of advances in AI. In this post, I argue for establishing perplexity (or, equivalently, cross-entropy loss) as a metric to compare between model developers and inform shared safety standards. As a first step, I provide more transparency into pretraining progress at OpenAI. Publishing our internal perplexity measurements took some consideration; it gives competitors a clearer picture of where we stand, but that concern is outweighed by the urgency of establishing shared ⁠ (opens in a new window) safety measures across the industry. A simplified, but fundamentally informative, view of AI development is that first, one builds a large computer, then uses it to pretrain a model (compressor) on a large unstructured dataset, then applies a variety of techniques—like prompting, finetuning or reinforcement learning—in different contexts to elicit the latent capabilities of that model. It would make little sense to evaluate GPT‑2 and GPT‑3 on most modern benchmarks; and the benchmarks used then were largely saturated around GPT‑4. 通胀方面，随着美国关税影响逐步退出同比数据、能源价格冲击减弱以及AI相关通胀压力缓和，全球通胀预计将在2027年放缓。不过，能源价格和存储芯片价格上涨仍可能带来通胀上行风险。货币政策方面，高盛预计多数主要经济体在2027年初仍将小幅收紧政策，随后部分央行有望于年内晚些时候逐步降息。具体而言，高盛预计美联储2026年12月和2027年3月加息，2027年12月降息；欧洲央行预计于2026年12月最后一次加息，并于2027年第四季度开始降息。

市场方面，高盛预计2027年股票和债券有望取得正回报，但AI投资周期变化、能源价格波动及融资成本上升可能加剧市场波动。该机构预计，标普500指数成分股盈利增速将从2026年的36%放缓至2027年的11%，并建议投资者通过多元化配置和风险对冲应对市场不确定性。

Anthropic公司或許已解決重大數學難題：黎曼猜想
展开全文（中文翻译）

在人工智能接連解決數學問題的一年裡，一名Anthropic員工表示，他可能已破解了數學界最重大的難題之一。

一名Anthropic員工兼數學家聲稱，他使用公司的人工智能聊天機器人，證明了著名的黎曼猜想（Riemann hypothesis）。

萊文特·阿爾波格（Levent Alpöge）在上週五表示，就在Anthropic於美國股市上市的隔天，他花了大約六週時間，使用Claude聊天機器人，為這個數學問題建立起詳細的證明。

阿爾波格形容這是「科學界某件更偉大事物的黎明」。他補充說，這套方法不僅可證明黎曼猜想，還能進一步解決被稱為廣義黎曼猜想（Generalized Riemann Hypothesis）的更大範圍問題。

此外，這項宣稱出現之際，OpenAI等AI公司近月來陸續宣布，已解決多項困擾學者數十年的高等數學難題。至於上週五提出的黎曼猜想證明，是使用Anthropic尚未公開的Claude版本完成的。

黎曼猜想是一道已有近170年歷史的數學問題，探討質數，並被廣泛認為是數學領域最著名的單一難題，主要原因在於其高度複雜性。
