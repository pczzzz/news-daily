What America Should Give to Make Peace in Ukraine

MICHAEL C. DESCH is Packey J. Dee Professor of International Relations at the University of Notre Dame and founding Brian and Jeannelle Brady Family Director of the O’Brien Notre Dame International Security Center.

In October, U.S. President Donald Trump sold his eleventh-hour deal to secure diesel from Russian President Vladimir Putin—pausing sanctions that his own administration had imposed—as a boon for the negotiations to end the war in Ukraine. The arrangement, he wrote on Truth Social, was intended to keep Russia at “the bargaining table.” Of course, it was always obvious to observers that the gambit had as much or more to do with bolstering the U.S. economy and stemming a massive victory for the Democrats in this month’s midterm elections, and in that, it failed.

Cynics have grounds for questioning all of Trump’s moves when it comes to Russia and Ukraine. He clearly dislikes Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and shows a marked preference for illiberal leaders, especially those willing to give him easy wins. Still, a stopped clock can be right twice a day. And concessions such as the fuel deal are exactly what might move Russia and Ukraine closer to peace, even if they are not wholly motivated by public-spiritedness.

Ukraine and its backers in Europe and the United States have long maintained that Russia only responds to sticks. The problem with their argument is that since 2014, and especially since 2022, Moscow has been subjected to nothing but sticks. According to Kaja Kallas, the European Union’s foreign policy chief, the more than 25,000 sanctions imposed on Russia after its full-scale invasion of Ukraine had cost its economy almost $1.5 trillion as of June. And although Trump has backed Ukraine less consistently than U.S. President Joe Biden did, especially in his rhetoric, his administration has still provided significant financial, military, and intelligence support; his CIA has been more aggressive than Biden’s in assisting Ukraine’s offensive and defensive air operations. All of these coercive measures have proved insufficient to compel a change in Putin’s policy or turn the tide in Ukraine’s favor on the battlefield.

European leaders may fret that Trump, now a lame duck and focused on his legacy, will push for a deal on Ukraine that suits the Kremlin’s interests more than their own. But ultimately, this remains a war in which Russia has the advantage. Europe should recognize that its pressure tactics have not worked and welcome the U.S. president’s willingness to throw in a few carrots among the many sticks used to try to beat the Kremlin into submission. Washington, meanwhile, should continue to pursue a concessionary strategy by encouraging Ukraine to surrender territory, freezing NATO’s expansion, and offering sanctions relief to Moscow. Putin has shown a willingness to sign favorable deals in the past, including the Minsk agreements in 2014 and 2015, which promised an end to the civil conflict in Donbas only to collapse under the duress of Kyiv and European capitals. A more enduring peace is desperately needed in Ukraine, however unfair the price may seem.

专家们对在军控方面取得重大突破的前景持谨慎和现实的态度，并表示要取得进展需要不止一次会议。尽管如此，深圳峰会为世界三大核大国之间的合作与对话提供了新的政治机会。事实上，三位领导人都强调了持续谈判的重要性，特朗普的声明对此进行了最好的总结：“接触机会的减少永远不会缔造和平。”

无核化可能会成为进一步接触的主题，因为它是特朗普三边峰会议程的重要议题。然而，尽管习近平重申应全面禁止和销毁核武器，但中国仍然拒绝与俄罗斯和美国签署军控协议，理由是两国核武库规模存在差距。因此，特朗普承认限制核武器将是困难的。

What follows is an attempt to reckon with the implications of AI for civil-military relations. For though in important ways AI is without technological precedent, its development is not the first time that a society has had to develop means of controlling an alien, useful and sometimes inscrutable body capable of great destruction. These parallels suggest ways of mitigating the risks to civil-military relations posed by this double-alignment problem.

Societies have long struggled to prevent misalignment with their militaries. The military profession survived such conflagrations because technology and geopolitics both necessitated it, but the question of how to mitigate the risk they represent has endured. IT之家 11 月 24 日消息，当地时间 11 月 23 日，Qt 团队发布了最新的集成开发环境（IDE）Qt Creator 21。

本次更新重点加强了 AI Agent 与 IDE 的协作能力，同时引入全新的 Profile 性能分析模式，并改进 C++/QML 调试、Git 版本控制、CMake 项目管理和代码编辑体验。

在 AI 功能方面，Qt Creator 21 默认启用 Agentic AI Chat（ACP）集成和 MCP 服务器，并为 MCP 服务器默认增加 Bearer Token 身份验证机制。外部 Agent 连接 Qt Creator MCP 时，需要在请求中添加授权令牌。

新版 MCP 服务器增加了多项工具，并允许用户在设置中分别配置这些工具的启用状态。Agentic AI Chat 也进行了升级，支持自动更新注册表中的 Agent、为此前使用过的工作区创建新会话，并显示单次会话及每轮交互的 Token 使用量和成本信息。此外，用户还可以切换 Agent、删除会话，并通过筛选和收藏功能更方便地选择模型。

性能分析方面，Qt Creator 21 新增 Profile 模式，集成全新的 Qt Profiler，取代此前的 QML Profiler。新版提供性能分析仪表板，可对应用程序的多项性能指标进行评分，并针对具体问题提供修复建议。分析轨迹可以作为独立文档打开，同时支持 Windows 和 macOS 平台的调用栈采样器，以及 Qt Tracepoints。

调试方面，新版增加了实验性的 C++/QML 联合调试引擎，可生成合并后的调用栈信息。用户可以在运行配置的 Debug 设置中同时选择 C++ 和 QML。此外，Qt Creator 21 还引入了模块化调试器后端，方便测试和扩展。
